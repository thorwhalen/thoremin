The chord map for a pentatonic scale's own five chords: open, folky harmony where nothing clashes.

:::details How it works
Instead of a key's seven chords, the map lays out the chords you get by stacking the pentatonic's own notes: in C, a C6, a Gsus4, an Asus4, a C and a Dsus4. These are the suspended, open sounds of folk and pop harmony. The five chords sit on five cells, and every step between neighbours reaches all five. The melody hand plays the notes of the chord you are on, which all come from the scale, so nothing clashes. Choose the pentatonic or tilt under Melody notes to play the whole scale.
:::

:::details Settings
In **Chord map**: the chords can come from the key (seven chords) or from the scale itself (here, five). The scale is chosen in **Sound**; with a pentatonic (major or minor) the map can use the scale's own five chords, and any other scale uses its key's seven.
:::
