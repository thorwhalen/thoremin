# ictus

Infer musical time (beat, phase, tempo, dynamics, articulation) from low-rate gesture. Pure TypeScript, causal, no framework, no clock, no DOM. In conducting, the *ictus* is the exact instant of the beat, the turning point where the gesture reverses; this module infers it from things that only imply it.

This directory is a package-in-waiting (thoremin #178 proposes extracting it as `ictus`): nothing in it imports from outside the directory except `@thoremin/stats` (the dependency-free order statistics, least-squares line and clamp it shares with the SDK's timing code, #392; it travels with ictus), and its tests import only from `@thoremin/ictus`. thoremin's `conductor` node, the body epic's `body-pulse` node and its audio pacer (#186) are its consumers.

## The shape

```
frames {t, channels} ──▶ [AnchorDetector] ──▶ Anchor ──▶ [RhythmPrior] ──▶ MusicalTime ──▶ consumers
   (stroke: one point; impact: several joints)
                                                 └──▶ [DynamicsEstimator] ──▶ dynamics, articulation
samples (t, x, y)  ──▶ [ImpactPredictor] ──▶ predict (early) / confirm (= Anchor) ──▶ [magnetise] ──▶ what to sound
```

- **Anchors are observations with likelihoods, never onsets.** The detector emits a beat *candidate* with a confidence; the prior decides whether it confirms the predicted beat, signals a tempo change, or is noise.
- **`AnchorDetector` is injected too** (#374): `push(ChannelFrame) -> Anchor | null`, `setPeriod`, `reset`. A `ChannelFrame` is `{ t, channels: { name: {x, y, z?, visibility?} }, scale? }`, so a one-point detector reads one channel and a multi-joint one reads several. `createIctus({ detector })` takes the stroke detector's options (it reads the `point` channel a bare `feed(sample)` is filed under) or an instance, the way it takes a prior; `feedFrame(frame)` feeds a whole frame. The stroke detector behind the seam is `createStrokeDetector({ channel })`, the same detector bit for bit.
- **`RhythmPrior` is injected, and three come by name** (`createRhythmPrior`, `IctusOptions.prior`): the adaptive oscillator (Large-Kolen circle map with Antescofo's adaptive attentional focus κ; robust to a stray anchor, 65 to 75 ms late under an accelerando), the trend prior (a least-squares tempo trend; no lag, carried by a stray anchor for its whole memory) and the **blend**, the default since #232: the oscillator's grid until three of its innovations in a row share a sign, then the trend's, back the same way; the trend half starts over whenever the oscillator re-seeds its lock or the two periods disagree by a fifth, and the published whole-beat count never steps back (`blend_prior.ts` has the argument and the numbers). `trend` by name is `trend12q` (`TREND_DEFAULTS`), the blend's own trend half. A Kalman filter or a multi-agent tracker fits behind the same `advance / update / predict / state` interface.
- **`MusicalTime` is the single source of truth.** Consumers evaluate it at their *own* time with `beatAt(state, t)` (the audio output timestamp, say), which is how the 30 Hz frame rate stops being the timing resolution.
- **Everything is relative to the player.** Stroke amplitude is normalised against the player's own recent envelope; articulation against their own usual braking sharpness. No calibration step, no absolute units.

## Use

```ts
import { createIctus } from '@thoremin/ictus';

const ictus = createIctus({ detector: { mirrorX: true }, oscillator: { beatsPerBar: 4 } });
// each frame, with the wrist (or index tip) of the beating hand:
const s = ictus.feed({ t, x, y });
s.tempo;        // bpm
s.phase;        // 0..1 within the beat
s.confidence;   // 0..1 — blend the beat-agnostic fallback against this
s.state;        // 'ready' | 'running' | 'hold'
s.dynamics;     // 0..1 from stroke size
s.articulation; // 0..1, legato (0) .. staccato (1)
```

`feedAnchor(anchor)` skips the detector for a consumer with its own anchor source (audio onsets, a pose-based dancer pulse). `advance(t)` free-runs between observations.

## Where the ictus is

The research map (§3) says: the peak deceleration of the beating hand as it brakes into the turning point, with the braking sharpness as the confidence. v1 fires on the **turning point itself** (the deepest vertical position of the stroke, confirmed by the first sample that has come back from it), timed below the frame period by a three-point parabolic fit on the *raw position samples*, and measures the braking sharpness separately as the articulation axis. The samples are not prefiltered: a median-of-three (the detector until #231, kept as `prefilter: 'median'`) replaces the bottom sample by a neighbour exactly where the fit needs it, which cost 5 ms and a frame on every sharp turn; instead a spike — a jump faster than a hand can move — is bridged by its neighbours, and the turn is confirmed by the first sample that comes back decisively (a few hundredths of the stroke) or by the second that is not deeper: one sample after a sharp bottom, two after a soft one, which is where the median confirmed every turn (`detector.ts` has the argument). The departure is deliberate: at 30 Hz the speed near a turning point is V-shaped, so a parabola fitted to the speed minimum is biased by about half a frame, whereas the position is smooth there and the fit is exact to a few milliseconds. `Anchor.confidence` is the stroke's amplitude relative to the player's envelope (a small stroke is a doubtful beat); a refractory window of a quarter period keeps the rebound from firing. The vertical-velocity zero crossing (every rule-based conducting system since the Radio Baton) is the selectable cheaper detector, `mode: 'zeroCrossing'`.

## The body's impact detector: anchors from several joints

`createImpactDetector` (`visual_impact.ts`, #374) reads several joints of a body and emits the beat candidates of the one that is beating. Its defaults are A1's measurement (`docs/research/body-conducting-and-pose-categories.md`), which corrected the design's multi-joint sum. Each joint's position is put in body units (divided by the running median of the frame's `scale`, the image torso length) and detrended by a trailing mean over a whole number of periods. Its envelope is the event: by default its own turning point (`extremum`, the bottom of its vertical travel: 31 of 32 significant cells on the real clips), or `deceleration` / `acceleration` (over the image plane, or the vertical alone with `axis: 'vertical'`; both fire at both ends of a stroke, about twice a beat) or `impact` (directional braking into the bottom, the bounce option). Peaks are counted in noise units: a rise of `peakMads` MADs above the lowest value since the last peak (a prominence, so a 4-pattern's small strokes count), confirmed once the envelope has fallen back by a quarter MAD and by twice its own frame-to-frame jitter (the confirmation that keeps a noisy bottom from firing twice), and a quarter period apart; the time is the vertex of the parabola through the peak and its neighbours at their real spacing (`parabolicVertex`, a dropped frame included). Each joint is weighted by its visibility, its stated weight and its periodicity: how its own recent peak intervals lock to the follower's period (their mean cosine in it, times the share of them under 1.5 periods, so an arm at the half-bar loses to a trunk at the beat once the follower's period is the beat; the followed joint's anchors set that period, so a level confirms itself unless the beat comes from elsewhere, the score's tempo say). `combine: 'select'` (the default) follows the best-weighted joint, switching for a clearly better rhythm, for a preferred joint with as good a rhythm (the wrist back from a gap), or when it has been unseen longer than a bridged gap (a dropped frame does not hand the beat over; two joints with no rhythm never trade it on noise); `combine: 'weighted'` is the design's sum of the noise-unit envelopes, weights to the fourth power. The anchor's `strength` (against that joint's own recent excursions, so a hand-over from wrist to trunk does not read as a diminuendo), `sharpness` and `lateral` are the followed joint's, as the stroke detector fills them. The default set is `BODY_IMPACT_JOINTS`: the beating `wrist`, the `trunk` centroid, the `elbow`, channels the caller fills.

On A1's conductor clips, through the follower, the default marks the beat with F 0.97, 0.81 and 1.00 (4, 3 and 2 patterns: the wrist alone scores the same, so the selection costs nothing); without the wrist, the trunk and the elbow lock to the hand's beat at R 0.81 and 0.61 (p below 10⁻³) on the 4- and 2-pattern clips, and the trunk alone does not on the slow 3-pattern; no dancer locks at the beat. `scripts/body/score_impact.ts` prints the table; `test/body_impact.test.ts` pins it.

## Impacts: predicting the strike before the frame that shows it

`impact.ts` is the second detector family: not "where is the beat in a conducting stroke" but "when will this stroke *hit*", answered before it does. A least-squares quadratic through the last approach samples is extrapolated to a plane — a known surface (`level`), or the player's own floor learned from confirmed reversals — and a prediction is committed once per stroke at the last sample that still leaves the consumer's required lead (`minLead`: audio output latency plus a frame), judging "last" by the gaps the samples have been coming at (a slow laptop's inference-paced 33 / 67 ms stamps, not just the last one). The reversal, when it is seen, confirms the stroke, and its departure tells a surface (a kink: approach and departure fits meet on the plane) from an air turn (a smooth vertex) so the floor and the braking lag are learned under the right rule. On the synthetic benchmark (`docs/research/subframe-impact-prediction.md`) a surface hit is predicted 43 ms early with a 6.6 ms spread against the frame-snapped detector's 10.8 ms one frame late; with the plane given, 5.4 ms.

`magnet.ts` pulls a predicted time toward a prior's nearest expected beat by a strength through the prior's attentional gate: the rhythmic twin of pitch magnetism, the dial between actuality and intent. `trend_prior.ts` is the second `RhythmPrior`, a least-squares tempo trend over the last anchors, for the case the oscillator lags: a player who is deliberately speeding up (the oscillator's grid runs 65 to 75 ms late under a 1.5 %-per-beat accelerando; the trend prior does not lag). `blend_prior.ts` is the third and the default: on the bench's accelerando its grid is 18 / 20 ms from the intended beat (surface / air) against the oscillator's 68 / 77 and the trend's 16 / 17, and at a steady tempo it is the oscillator within 0.2 ms. The air drum consumes the predictor and the conductor's grid. `lagGate` (#239) decides how much of a pull a grid has earned: the instrument keeps the last eight strokes' `confirmed impact - grid at commit`, and the pull is scaled by `max(0, 1 - |mean| / (3 se))`, so a grid sitting further off the strokes than their own scatter explains (a lagging follower) pulls nothing.

## The pulse: musical time from a continuous movement

`pulse.ts` (#186 PR F) is the third input path, for a mover who does not mark every beat: a dancer. `createPulseFollower().feed(t, value)` takes one continuous channel (the body node feeds a stride, a head height or a hip height, in torso lengths) and returns a `PulseState`, which is a `MusicalTime` whose "beat" is one cycle of the movement, plus `level` (the ACF's period), `strength` and every ACF peak as `candidates`. A causal autocorrelation over the last 8 s (detrended by a moving average as long as the longest searchable period, normalised per lag by the overlapping segments' energies) chooses the level; a least-squares sinusoid with a drift term measures it and gives its phase; each fitted maximum, once a quarter cycle in the past, is an `Anchor` for a `RhythmPrior` (any of the three by name, or an instance).

**No octave is assumed.** A peak within 6 % of the current level is that level; any other peak is a different level whatever its ratio, and takes over only by persisting for 1.5 s. Among peaks within 90 % of the strongest the shortest is the fundamental, because a signal that repeats every P also repeats every 2P and 3P: that is the definition of a period, not a fold toward a beat range. The measured reason is the Que Calor dancer at 129.2 bpm: the stride repeats once per bar (1.86 s), not per beat, and a choreography may as well repeat at three or six beats, which no power of two reaches. A level is reported only above an ACF strength of 0.4 (noise reaches 0.2 to 0.35 at long lags under the per-lag normalisation; the stride sits at 0.76), and the prior runs on a clock delayed by the anchors' settle, so its confidence does not decay between anchors that are merely late. Relating the pulse to a song's grid is the consumer's job. On synthetic cycles from 0.5 s to 1.86 s the period is within 2 % and the anchors and the phase within a quarter frame (`test/ictus_pulse.test.ts`); on the dancer, the stride's level is on the bar in every confident tick after 6 s and its anchors' resultant against the bar grid is 0.90 (`test/body_pulse.test.ts`).

## The song's grid: musical time from a song's audio

`song_grid.ts` (#186 PR G) is the other half of following a dancer: when the SONG repeats. It is offline, not causal (a loaded song is known in full), and returns a `SongGrid` (the paces ruler: a least-squares line `phaseS + k * periodS`, the tracked beats beside it, a confidence, and an empty `downbeats` until a downbeat tracker exists). `trackSongGrid(mono, { sampleRate })` is a two-band onset envelope (log-energy increase of the signal and of its first difference), a tempo from the autocorrelation of a lightly smoothed envelope weighted by a broad log-normal prior around 120 bpm (the octave tie-break), a phase from a comb against the low band only (so hi-hat eighths and swing do not pull it), and beats walked outward from the middle of the song, each snapped to the strongest onset within a tenth of a period, so a drifting song keeps its own beats. `songTimeAt(grid, position, { t, rate, playing })` reads the song as a `MusicalTime` with tempo and the next beat in ENGINE time, so it compares with a `PulseState` directly. On synthetic click tracks (60 to 150 bpm, hi-hat eighths, swing) the tempo is within 0.2 bpm and 95 % of beats within 50 ms (`test/ictus_song_grid.test.ts`); the remaining failure is the octave of a fast song, which the pace controller absorbs by relating levels over integer ratios.

## Pacing a song to a dancer

`pace.ts` (#186 PR H) closes the loop between the two: `createPaceController().step({ t, pulse, song, rate, base })` takes the dancer's `PulseState`, the song's `MusicalTime` (`songTimeAt`, engine time, possibly a tick old) and the rate the song was played at, and returns the rate to play it at now, with a `mode` (`idle`, `waiting`, `locking`, `following`, `holding`), the `ratio`, the phase error and the nudge. The relation is an integer ratio of song beats per dancer cycle (`1..8` and `1/2..1/8`), the one whose matching rate is nearest the player's own tempo (`base`), and none when that one is more than 15 % away (`relatePeriods`). A ratio is followed only after it has persisted 3 s, the first one and every change: on low-passed noise the pulse follower is trusted on about 2 % of ticks, and without the persistence the controller would follow every one of them; with it, none (`test/ictus_pace.test.ts`). The song's own period is the average of the last 8 s, not the one beat interval at the playhead (a tracked beat is snapped up to a tenth of a period). Then D-Jogger's strategy 4: the matching rate smoothed (1 s), clamped to `base` +-10 % inside the player's own 0.5 to 2, and nudged by 3 % while the dancer's cycle start and a song beat are more than a twelfth of a beat apart, released under a 48th. Below the pulse's trust threshold the rate holds. Only the followed level is related, not every `candidates` entry, because only it carries a phase. On the Que Calor dancer, through the real wiring, the dancer's anchors land in the song's beat with R = 0.67 to 0.71 whether the song starts 6 % slow, on tempo or 6 % fast (0.09 to 0.45 unfollowed), and the rate never leaves the clamp (`test/pace_controller.test.ts`).

## Scheduling a score on the follower's time

`scheduler.ts` (#187 PR 5) is the research map's pending-event scheduler (§6.3, §7.3): a score's notes in beats become note-on / note-off commands with times, each committed only once its predicted time `now + (B - beat) / (bpm / 60)` falls inside a short horizon (the output stage plus two engine ticks in practice), and never moved after. Held (`bpm` 0) commits nothing ahead and releases nothing sounding, so a fermata note sustains; a note the beat has passed entirely is skipped, one under way starts now; one voice per key and channel (unison parts merge, a re-struck key is released a hair before the new onset, and an onset never lands on or before its key's committed release); a rewind releases everything; releases are lazy, so the tempo keeps shaping a held note until just before it ends. The caller maps the times to its output clock; thoremin's `orchestra` node does it through the one clock map. Hold detection and the preparatory beat stay in the follower (the `conductor` node freezes the beat); the KTH ritard prior at low confidence is not in v1.

## What v1 leaves for later

The map's `predict(t)`, gains scaled by the inter-beat interval, and `Anchor.kind`. None is needed by the first consumers; each is an additive change behind the same interface. `MusicalTime.leadOffset` (the conductor's beat leads the sound by a per-person constant) exists as a field since #232; nothing measures it yet (a trainer cue will).

## Testing

Synthetic multi-joint bodies (`test/helpers/body_bounce.ts`: a stroking wrist, its elbow, a bouncing trunk, exact beats) pin the impact detector and the seam (`test/ictus_impact_detector.test.ts`: lock within three beats, an accelerando, a lost joint, camera distance, the stroke detector behind the seam bit for bit). Synthetic ictus trains pin the oscillator (lock in three beats, accelerando, dropped and doubled anchors, a rebound that must not flip the tempo, a half-period seed that must recover, hold and preparatory restart) and the blend (at rest it is the oscillator to the bit; the accelerando's grid error; the false-trigger rate at a steady humanised tempo; a rebound and a dropped beat with the trend engaged). Real conducting-pattern excerpts at a *stated* 70 bpm (`test/fixtures/conducting_*/`) pin the detector and the follower's predictions: `metrics.ts` ports the `mir_eval` F-measure, Cemgil accuracy and CMLt continuity, and `fitGrid` builds the reference grid from the stated tempo alone (only the phase is fitted), so the excerpts are ground truth without hand annotation. The tests import the fixture loader and the `HandsFrame` type from the repo; the core itself imports nothing from outside this directory but `@thoremin/stats`.
