# @thoremin/ext-faces

Personal categorizers for thoremin: faces, hand shapes (#385 E8) and body poses (#382) ([#330](https://github.com/thorwhalen/thoremin/issues/330)). A player records a take of their own faces; the take is carved into categories (as many as they choose); each category is shown by its faces and named, with defaults from the general expression model matched one-to-one; several categorizers are saved, and an instrument's face chords read one in place of the general model.

This package is phases P2 (the session), P3 (test, correct, tune, import and export), P4 (an instrument chooses its categorizer and the chord each category plays), P5 (shared categorizers) and P6 (the general model is one categorizer) of #330.

## In the app

Tools, then **Your faces**, then **Record my faces**, which opens **Explore** (#385 E2): the camera is read (the face features claimed, the instrument quiet) and nothing is recorded. Two things show what the camera reads:

- **The bars**: a handful of features, each centred on your rest and measured in noise units (how far a feature moved, in multiples of its own frame-to-frame jitter: the units the carve uses, so a bar that barely moves is a feature the carve cannot use either). Before the rest they are measured from the last moments. Exploring, they are the features that moved most lately; once your holds make two or more categories, they are the features that tell those apart, with a coloured tick at each category so far (numbered by its first hold), and the faint band is your rest's own spread. *more* shows twelve.
- **The ring**: it fills while a face is held, closes when the hold is taken, and asks for a new face while that one is already given.

**Bursts** (#385 E7). While capturing or paused, hold **B** (or the *Hold to record a burst* button) to record: every frame while it is held becomes ONE held face (its mean, the frames thinned as its support), in a capture segment of its own. Type a name first and the burst is a **labelled** face: the carve puts it in the category of that name (joining one already called that, case aside; `neutral` is the Neutral bin), pinned, so it stays there at every k. Paused, bursts are the only capture (burst mode); capturing, a burst drops the hold in progress and capture resumes after it without taking the burst's face again. A burst shorter than five frames takes nothing and says so. Its picture is taken a few frames in, while the face is still being made. Pause, Resume or Stop with B still held end the burst first (it is kept). The name is the take's: a new take starts without one. A named burst merged into a saved categorizer (*Add more of my faces*) joins its category the same way, and one burst is enough for a merge.

**Start capturing** (or **Space**) records: a few seconds of rest, then each face you want the instrument to know, holding each for a moment. **Pause** (or Space) stops collecting while you think of the next face: the bars and the ring keep running (the ring says what *would* be taken), and nothing is taken inside a pause or across one. **Resume** (or Space) carries on. Space is the command `carve.capture.toggle` (also in the palette, and for a gesture or the assistant); it only acts while a carve is live, so elsewhere Space is the page's. Four clicks from a cold load to recording: Tools, Your faces, Record my faces, Start capturing. **Stop and carve** shows the grid:

- **Neutral**, taught by example (#385 E5). The rest take puts your resting faces in the **Neutral** bin, and you can drag any face into it ("this face means nothing"); a face nearest your Neutral faces reads as none of these and plays nothing. k counts only your expressions. One switch says what a face you never taught reads as: *Neutral* (the default: a face far from every category is none of these) or *The nearest face* (it reads as whichever category it is closest to, or Neutral when it is closer to your Neutral faces). The switch moves no face and re-cuts nothing. If every held face was close to your resting face, all of them are carved and the notice asks you to move the resting ones to Neutral.
- **How many**: a slider (and - / +) starting at the suggested k. Moving it re-divides the same take, with no new recording. It never goes below the categories you placed holds in (#385 E4).
- **Names**: one click on a name to edit it. A name you typed is kept when k changes (it anchors its category: the category's most typical face is pinned to it); the others are proposed again, one-to-one, by the general model (`neutral` for a resting category in a file made before #385 E5, `expression n` when nothing fits).
- **Pictures**: every held face, per category, the most typical first, then by distance from the category's centre (a long category shows 8 and a *+n*). Each held face gets its picture the moment it is taken (#385 E3): the live camera frame, cropped to your face (a box around the nose tip, sized by the span of the eyes), kept in memory until you save; then every held face's picture is filed in IndexedDB under that hold's id.
- **Sorting** (#385 E4: one editable carve). From Stop on, the take IS a categorizer, the same document a reopened or imported one is, so everything below works on both. Every held face can be moved, three ways: drag its picture onto a category or a bin; click pictures to select them and press a category's number (1-9), **N** (Neutral), **S** (Set aside) or **Delete**, or use the bar of buttons that appears; or the menu under the picture. **Neutral** holds are the taught "none of these" (in a categorizer made before #385 E5 they are not trained on until you press *Teach Neutral from these*); **Set aside** keeps holds without training on them ("not sure yet"); **Delete** removes them (a category left empty goes too). Every move is a **pin**: a re-cut of k keeps a moved face where it was put, a merged pair merged and a named category named (pinned faces show *pin*). A category's **More** menu merges it into another or **splits it in two** (its own faces cut at 2); dragging a category's header onto another merges them too. **Undo / Redo** (or Cmd/Ctrl-Z, Cmd/Ctrl-Shift-Z inside the grid) step through every edit and restore the exact previous categorizer. The pure edits are the SDK's `enroll/sorting` (the constrained re-cut is `buildConstrainedForest` in `enroll/cluster`).

- **Quality** (#385 E6). Each category carries a chip: three dots and a word, *distinct*, *ok*, *close to X* (another category, or Neutral) or *needs another hold*; its tooltip gives the numbers (how many times its own spread its nearest neighbour is, below 2 is close; the share of its faces that sit nearest their own category). A face that sits nearer another category, or Neutral, than its own gets a **?** and a *like X?* button: one click moves it there (a pin, undoable). A close category gets a hint in plain words, "\"open smile\" is close to \"smile\". They differ mostly in mouth open (+9.9). To tell them apart, open your mouth wider for \"open smile\", or merge them.", with a *Merge* button. It never shows a face to imitate.
- **Save** under a name. Saving under an existing name replaces that categorizer, its pins included. Saved ones can be reopened (and re-cut: the hierarchy is rebuilt from the samples, around the pins) or deleted.

Nothing leaves the browser. No video of the take is recorded at all: only a small cropped still per held face, and only a saved categorizer's are kept.

### Test, correct and tune a saved one (P3)

**Open** a saved categorizer (or **Import file**). Under its grid:

- **Test with my face**: a live readout of what it reads. The settled category (with a short hysteresis), each category's membership, a *Neutral* bar (the taught class's share), and *None of these* when a face is nearest your Neutral faces or beyond the reject radius. The instrument stays quiet while testing.
- **The margin meter** (#385 E6), above the bars: how deep inside its nearest category (or Neutral) the face you hold is, full on the category's centre and empty on the boundary with the runner-up: *safely inside X*, *on the edge with Y*, or *outside every category* (past the reject radius). A face that only ever reaches the edge is one to make bigger, or to merge.
- **Correct it**. *This face is X* adds the face you are holding (the last quarter second, raw) to X as a new sample. The menu under each picture moves that face (see Sorting above). Both are pins. A category keeps at least one face (merge it, or delete its faces). Every correction re-derives the model from the samples (`modelFromCategorizer`), so it is a retrain, not a patched centroid.
- **Add faces**. *Add more of my faces* records a short take and merges it in (P1's `mergeSession`, seed `samples`): each new face joins its nearest category and everything already there stays. *Retrain on my face* (seed `centroids`) keeps only which category is which and learns every category again from your take; it is for a categorizer someone else made.
- **Tune** (at classification time, no retraining; `src/lib/live.ts`). *Sensitivity* scales how far a face is from your resting face (the Neutral faces; the `neutral` category of an older file whose every face was a category) before it is read: above 1 a smaller face reaches its category. *Reject radius* scales the distance beyond which a face is none of these. Its base is floored, so x1 always accepts at least nine in ten of the categorizer's own faces even when a file carries a tiny radius. When a face you never taught reads as the nearest face it is clamped out (nothing is ever rejected). *Distance* takes out what camera distance does to each feature, as the categorizer's own samples show it: the slope of each feature against log face scale within each category, kept only for the features distance explains well (r² at least 0.5). A take recorded at one distance teaches nothing, and the dial says so.
- **Size of each face** (#385 E7; Project Gameface's "gesture size"): one dial per category, how big a face it takes to count as it. Every distance to that category is multiplied by it before any comparison (the other categories, Neutral, the reject radius; `Category.scale`, read by `classify` and the margin meter), so below 1 a smaller face reaches it ("I can make frown, but only a small one") and above 1 a face that fires by accident needs to be bigger. A tuning like the others: kept with the record, read when an instrument plays it, never in the file. Kept by category id with the category's name, and applied only while that id still carries that name (a re-cut can give an id to another face; a renamed category starts at 1).
- **Check it** (#385 E7):
  - **Quiz me**: each category named twice in a random order (never the same twice in a row), a 2 s relax before each (it does not say which face comes next, and a face already held when the prompt comes counts only once let go and made again: the quiz measures finding a face, not holding one); made within 2 s and held for 1 s is *found*, made later (before 5 s) is *slow*, never is *missed* (with what it read instead). The result per category (asked, found, slow, missed, typical time, what a miss read as) is kept with the categorizer on Save and shown beside its quality chip (*found 1 of 2*), only while the category keeps its id and name. A missed or slow face offers *Record more X*: a merge take with the burst name set to X. *Keep the found faces as samples* adds each found face (its held second) as a correction.
  - **Everyday check**: a minute of talking and looking around, making none of the faces on purpose; it counts each category's entries (Neutral never counts) and flags one firing at least once a minute: *surprise fired 6 times while you did other things: make it bigger, set its size above 1, or merge it*. Kept with the categorizer too (*fired 6x* beside the chip).
  Both are local only: kept on the saved record on this device, never in the exported file, never sent anywhere. The pure parts are the SDK's `enroll/quiz` and `enroll/everyday`, modality-free.
- **Export file**: P1's versioned file (`thoremin.categorizer`, samples, names, feature space), saved as `<name>.categorizer.json`. It is on a fresh carve beside Save (exported under the name typed for Save, else "My faces"; exporting does not save), on every saved row (**Export**, without opening it), and in an open categorizer's test and tune section (#369). No pictures and no tuning: the tuning is about this room and camera, so it stays with the saved record on this device. **Import file** validates, migrates an older version, refuses a newer one, and opens it unsaved. A file carries no pictures; a face this device already has a picture of (the file was exported here) shows it, since pictures are filed by the hold's id.

*Not saved yet* marks a carve, a correction, a merge, an import or a tuning that is not saved; Record, Open, Import and Close the grid ask before dropping it.

Lighting gets no dial: [`docs/research/face-categorizer-lighting-and-distance.md`](../../docs/research/face-categorizer-lighting-and-distance.md) has what was measured.

### Play it: an instrument chooses its categorizer (P4)

In the instrument editor (**Edit** an instrument), the **Face categorizer** section:

- **Reads your face with**: *The general model* (the default, so nothing changes for an existing instrument) or one of your saved categorizers. Choosing one turns the face chords on if the face played nothing yet, and puts every category's chord back to Auto. Three clicks from a cold load: Edit, the section, the categorizer.
- **Or from the tool**: Tools, then Your faces, then **Play it** beside a saved categorizer (#357). It makes the same single `dial.patch` as the select (`chooseCategorizerWrites`) on the current instrument, which is left unsaved like any edit. Once the instrument names it, the row says *Playing* (or *Chosen (face chords off)* when the face plays timbre), since choosing it again would only put its chords back to Auto.
- **Under the section**, one line says what the face chords read with right now: *Reading with* your categorizer, *Loading…*, *Not on this device: the general model plays*, or *The general model*. (The host shows extension readouts under an instrument's row only for the air and sign instruments, so the section is where this one lives; #367.)
- **The chord each of your faces plays**: *Auto* plays the chord this instrument gives the expression the face stands in for (its one-to-one match with the general model, the same match that named it; a face the general model does not recognise takes an expression no other face took). Or pick a degree, or *Silence*.

What plays:

- A face nearest your Neutral faces, or beyond the reject radius for a moment, is *none of these* and plays what `neutral` plays: silence by default, like a resting face on the general model. In an older file whose every face was a category, the category named `neutral` (that carve's name for the resting face) stands in for `neutral`; rename it and it is a face like any other.
- The instrument keeps the categorizer's **id**, not the model. The model stays in your collection on this device, with its tuning (P3). An instrument that names a categorizer this device does not have plays the general model, and the section says so. A correction saved in the tool reaches the instrument at once.
- The chord choices are kept per category PLACE (`cat-1`, `cat-2`, ...): re-carving or merging into the categorizer can renumber its faces, so check them after one.
- Silence, not hold: a face nearest Neutral for 3 camera frames (Neutral is a class like the others: the entry hysteresis), or beyond the reject radius for 8 (about a quarter second), lets the chord go, so a resting face is quiet as on the general model; a blink is not. The node param `holdOnReject` keeps the last chord instead.
- The general model stays in the graph under yours: it plays until yours has loaded, and whenever the dial names a categorizer that is not there.

How it is built: the dial `faceCategorizer` (`src/schema.ts`: `id`, and a degree per category slot `cat-1`..`cat-12`, Auto by default) composes the branch `face-categorizer`, which **replaces** core's `face-chord` (rule 5 of `composeGraph`) and feeds core's chord renderer (`expression-chord`) the same `face-expression` kind with the player's categories on it (`ExpressionScores.personal`). The node computes the face vector itself from the face frame (no feature claim), and the model arrives on the transient `faceCategorizerModel`, resolved off the tick by the React half's mount effect (`src/app/categorizerPlay.ts`). The node's code loads the first time a composed graph names it.

### Shared categorizers (P5)

Categorizers shipped with the app for every player, listed under **Shared** in *Reads your face with* (the same three clicks from a cold load).

- Each is P1's file, `public/categorizers/<name>.json`, listed in `public/categorizers/index.json` (a label, a line on what it is, and `demo` for a demonstration). The id is `shared:<name>`; a saved categorizer's id is a slug of its name, so the two never collide. The files are fetched the first time the section opens or an instrument names one, so none is in any bundle chunk.
- A shared file that cannot be fetched (offline, a deploy in flight) plays the general model, and the section says so with a **Try again**; a failed fetch is never kept.
- A shared file's categories are given the dial's slot ids (`cat-1`, ...) in order on load, so each one can have its own chord.
- A shared categorizer plays **untuned**: the P3 tuning is about one player's room and camera. **Copy to my categorizers** saves it among your own (under a name none of yours has, by name or by the id the name slugs to, so nothing is replaced) and points the instrument at the copy, keeping the chords you chose. Open the copy in Tools, then Your faces, to tune it or *Retrain on my face*. A copy, not a per-device tuning keyed by the shared id: the tuning dials only make sense next to the live test, which is in the tool, and a model that does not transfer from one face to another needs the retrain anyway, which needs a copy.
- The one shipped today is a **demo** (`demo-smile`), built by `npm run faces:demo` from the repo's own three-second test recording (`test/fixtures/video_face_expressions`) and checked against it by a test. The real shared categorizers are recorded and exported in the app (Tools, Your faces, Export file) and added beside it with an index entry.

### The general model is one categorizer (P6)

The general (shipped) expression model is a categorizer like the others, with the id `general`:

- Its categories are the seven expressions (`happy`, `sad`, `angry`, `surprised`, `fearful`, `disgusted`, `kiss`), each standing in for itself, and `neutral` is its "none of these". Core's `face-expression` emits its readings in the categorizer shape (`ExpressionScores.personal`, from the SDK's `generalReading`).
- The chord renderer and the overlay read only that shape, whichever categorizer made the reading. There is no second, closed-label path. A reading recorded before P6 (no `personal`) is read as the general one (`categorizerReading`).
- Its per-category degrees are the instrument's expression degrees (`faceExpr.degrees`). Each category stands in for itself, so its Auto degree is that map, and no second copy of the map exists. No persisted shape changed, so there is no store migration.
- Instruments play exactly as before: `test/faces/general_categorizer.test.ts` replays the real recording and a scripted face holding all seven expressions. It runs under two degree maps, through `face-chord` and through `face-categorizer` with the dial on `general` or on a missing id, and checks a golden hashed from main before the change.
- The general model stays core's node, not the extension's `face-categorizer`. A build without this extension (`npm run test:core`) must still play the face chords, and an extension node is loaded lazily.

`?faces.source=synthetic` runs the session on a scripted face with a canvas video: no camera and no face model. It is a verification affordance, and the browser smoke drives it.

### Your hand shapes (#385 E8)

The same tool over one hand's SHAPE: Tools, then **Your hand shapes**, then **Record my hand shapes**, then **Start capturing** (four clicks from a cold load, as for faces). Everything above (Explore, the bars and the ring, pause, bursts, the carve, Neutral, sorting, quality, the quiz, the everyday check, test and tune, import and export) is the same code, in the hand's words.

- **What it reads** (#387 C4): one hand, side-relative, so a shape made with either hand is the same category. **Which hand** (above the take, and in the grid of a carve or a saved one) says *Either hand* (the default; the right one when both are in view), *My left hand only* or *My right hand only*; it is kept on the file (`hand`) and the live test and the bindings read that hand. Only the shape counts: the features are the hand's flexion, spread, pinch and openness that the catalog declares invariant to where the hand is and how it is turned, the same space as the air guitar's chord shapes (`handShapeFeatureIds` in the SDK), so a carved hand sample is an air guitar vocabulary vector.
- **The rest** is a relaxed hand held up to the camera; it seeds Neutral as the resting face does.
- **Names**: the general model is the hand's three rule poses (*open*, *fist*, *pinch*: openness, the index finger's bend and the thumb-index distance), matched one-to-one; the others are `shape n`.
- **Pictures**: each held shape's still is cropped to the hand (a square around the wrist and the five fingertips) with a skeleton drawn over it (the wrist through the palm to each fingertip), so it shows what the camera read.
- **Play it: Bind to commands**. A saved hand categorizer's row has **Bind to commands** (no *Play it*: the face chords read faces). It hands the saved version to the host's category bindings (the Gestures tool's *Your categories*, through the SDK-UI seam `categoryBinder()`), as a set that reads the live vector through the hand (`read: { modality: 'hand', hand }`), and opens the Gestures tool on it, where each category gets a command. Three clicks from a cold load: Tools, Your hand shapes, Bind to commands. Binding again under the same name replaces the set and keeps its commands by category name. The bound set is a copy: re-bind after an edit (#385 E10 keys bindings by categorizer id).

`?carve.source=synthetic` runs it on a scripted hand (`src/lib/synthetic_hand.ts`: an articulated right hand going through open, fist, point and pinch with a relaxed hand between), with no camera and no hand model; `?faces.source=synthetic` is still read as the same switch.

### Your poses (#382 = #385 E9)

The same tool over held body POSES (#387 B8: static poses; a wave or a clap is a different classifier): Tools, then **Your poses**, then **Record my poses**, then **Start capturing** (four clicks from a cold load, as for faces and hand shapes). Everything above is the same code, in the body's words (`bodyModality` in `@thoremin/sdk/features/modalities/body`).

- **What it reads**: B1's pose space (`bodyPoseCollects()`: joint angles, segment directions in the torso's own frame, body-relative positions, shape ratios; nothing that says where the player stands). The tool claims those groups through feature demand under its own owner (`poses`, and `poses-test` for the live test), which is what composes the body branch: the pose model runs only while a pose tool reads it.
- **The setup**: the rest ("stand or sit facing the camera, relaxed, arms at your sides"), then three short nuisance demonstrations, each about four seconds and each skippable: closer and further, a step aside, a slight turn. Whatever moves during them is down-weighted (`weightsFromNuisance`), so where the player stands is not what they do. A take replayed from a recording that never demonstrated them skips them.
- **Cannot see you**: a body counts as in view only when it shows `BODY_POSE_MIN_COVERAGE` (80 %) of the features its categorizer was TRAINED on (`CarveModality.minCoverage`, `inView`); the visibility mask drops a hidden joint's features, so a player half out of frame reads *Cannot see you* in the live test instead of a guess. The category dispatcher's own presence bar (90 %) applies to a bound set.
- **The mirror** (#387 B10): off by default, chosen before the take (*Left and right are the same pose*), kept on the file (`mirror: true`) and on a bound set's `read`. With it on, every vector is read on a canonical side (of a pose and its mirror image, the one whose left side is the more raised and outward), so "left arm out" and "right arm out" are one pose in the take, the live test and the bindings. It cannot be changed after the take: the holds were read on that side.
- **Pictures** (#387 C7): each hold is a STICK FIGURE drawn from its segment directions (`poseSkeleton`: what the carve measured, no picture of anyone), kept per sample in the file (`skeleton`), so an imported or shared categorizer shows what each category looks like. Camera stills are opt-in (*Also keep camera pictures*) and are the whole frame.
- **Names** (#383): default names by context, from a hand-written prototype categorizer over the pose space (`@thoremin/sdk/features/modalities/body_names`): the standing set (`rest`, `arms up`, `left arm up`, `right arm up`, `arms out`, `hands together`, `lean left`, `lean right`, `crouch`), the seated set (the same minus `crouch`, chosen when the take never saw the legs) or the conductor's (`ready`, `fermata`, `cut-off`, `rest`, chosen before the take or asked for by the Conductor tool's "Teach my conducting poses"; kept on the file as `vocabulary`). With the mirror on, the sided names merge (`one arm up`, `lean`). One-to-one by `matchLabels`; a category no rule reaches is `pose n`.
- **Play it: Bind to commands**, as a hand categorizer (three clicks from a cold load). The conductor's transport from pose categories is B4 ([#384](https://github.com/thorwhalen/thoremin/issues/384)).

`?carve.source=synthetic` runs it on a scripted body (`src/lib/synthetic_body.ts`: the graph's synthetic skeleton at rest, the three nuisances, then arms up, left arm out, right arm out and a crouch with the rest between), with no camera and no pose model.

### One collection for every categorizer (#385 E8, #387 C5)

Every saved categorizer, face, hand or body, is a record of ONE zodal named collection, `thoremin.categorizers`, carrying its file's `modality` (`src/app/categorizers.ts`). Each tool, the editor section and the instrument's resolver read a view of one modality. A face categorizer keeps the id it had (the slug of its name, which an instrument's `faceCategorizer` dial names); another modality's ids are prefixed (`hand:fist`), so a hand shape and a face may share a name.

This is the EXPAND step: the first time a view is used on a device, the old face collection (`thoremin.faceCategorizers`) is copied in (migrated as a saved record always is) and a flag (`thoremin.categorizers.migrated`) says so, so a categorizer deleted later is never copied back. The old key is left untouched for a rollback; #385 E10 removes it. Stills stay in the IndexedDB store they were in (`thoremin-faces`), keyed by hold id, which every modality shares.

## Shape

A **lazy** extension ([`docs/design/lazy-extensions.md`](../../docs/design/lazy-extensions.md)). The eager stubs are `index.ts` (the dial, the branch as data, the transient, the derivation; its node type named in `provides`) and `ui.tsx` (the tool and the editor section, as data). The node is `lazy.ts`, loaded the first time a composed graph names it; the React half is `lazy_ui.tsx`, loaded with it or the first time the tool or the section opens.

| `src/app/categorizerPlay.ts` | the dial's id resolved to a model off the tick, published on the transient |

The pure pieces that hands will want too are in the SDK. `@thoremin/sdk/enroll/carve` holds `createCarver` and `neutralIndices`. P1 provides the matcher, the exemplars and the file.

**Holds, stills and the file's version 2 (#385 E3).** Every held face is a sample with a stable id, `<takeId>:<n>`. A category's members, the `neutral` bin (the "none of these" faces) and the `aside` bin (kept, not trained) name samples by id, and every sample is in exactly one of them; `strangers` (`neutral` or `nearest`) says what a face far from every category reads as (the file's form of the reject / partition switch). Stills are filed under the same ids, so a merge keeps the base's samples, ids and stills untouched and files only the new holds' pictures, and a still is deleted only when no saved record names its hold. Each sample also keeps up to four supporting frames from its hold, stored as compact rows over the categorizer's features (rounded to five places), as evidence; the model does not read them yet. A version-1 file migrates on import; a version-1 saved record migrates on load with its samples given its old still keys (`<takeId>:<index>`) as ids, so nothing is re-filed (`test/faces/categorizer_v2_migration.test.ts` checks every shipped and fixture file: the derived model is byte-identical, and the migration is lossless).

**Neutral as a class, and the file's version 3 (#385 E5).** `neutralTrained` says whether the `neutral` holds are trained as a null class (the SDK's `trainNeutral`: up to four prototypes, split where the Neutral faces sit farther apart than a category is wide, since a resting face and a look at the keyboard are both "nothing"). A live face nearest a prototype reads as no category (`Classification.neutral`), in either setting of `strangers`; the tracker enters it through the same hysteresis as a category and never holds through it. Files of versions 1 and 2 migrate with `neutralTrained: false`, so they classify exactly as before (`test/faces/classify_golden.test.ts`, a golden recorded before the change); the Sorting grid offers *Teach Neutral from these*, an undoable edit. A merged take sorts a new face nearest Neutral into Neutral. The shipped demo is version 3 (its version-2 form is kept as `test/faces/fixtures/demo_smile.v2.categorizer.json`).

**Quality (#385 E6).** The numbers are the SDK's `enroll/quality`, pure and modality-free, every distance in noise units: `carveQuality` (per category the separation ratio to its nearest neighbour, Neutral's prototypes being one neighbour, over its own spread, CLOSE below 2; leave-one-hold-out against its centre without the hold, so the share of its holds nearest their own centre and where the rest leak; the suspect holds, Neutral's included, by the centroid silhouette `(b - a) / max(a, b)` < 0), `qualityHint` (the movements that separate a pair, folded and worded by the modality's `describe`, with the instruction that moves the category away) and `liveMargin` (`(d2 - d1) / (d2 + d1)` and `d1 / rejectRadius`). The face's words are `features/modalities/face_words.ts`: one table by movement (a mouth opening is six features; an eye aspect ratio reads it backwards), with head turns said without a side, since a hint that says left for the wrong side is worse than none. A hand or body modality gets its hints by implementing `describe`. `sortingView` carries `quality`, `hints` and `suspects`; `LiveReading` carries `margin`.

**The modality seam (#385 E1).** Everything face-specific the take, the carve, the live tuning and the session store need is read through one `CarveModality` (`@thoremin/sdk/enroll/modality`): the groups to claim, how to read the modality's vector from the merged live vector, which keys a sample keeps, the general model, the distance proxies and the words. The face is `faceModality` (`@thoremin/sdk/features/modalities/face`); the app half (crop region, preview, play targets, the claim and hush) is `@thoremin/sdk-ui/enroll/modality`. Each shared module takes the modality as an option defaulting to the face, so hands and body (E8, E9) pass theirs and fork nothing. `test/faces/carve_modality.test.ts` guards both sides: the shared modules import no `features/face_*` module, and the face through the seam is the face as it was (cues word for word, the same keys, names and import refusals).
