## Thoremin

![Thoremin, with the instrument list open](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/home.jpg)

Thoremin turns your camera into a musical instrument. Hold a hand up and it plays; make a face and it plays chords; mime a drum, a bass, a guitar or a flute and it plays those; conduct an orchestra or dance to a song. It runs in your browser: nothing you play is uploaded.

### Start in one minute

1. Open [the camera instruments](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/instruments/) in Chrome (other browsers work with a few gaps: see [troubleshooting](#troubleshooting)) and allow the camera.
2. Press **Tap to play**. Browsers need a tap before they will make any sound.
3. Hold a hand up. Move it left and right for pitch, up and down for loudness. Raise a second hand for a second voice.

No camera? Add `?slot.source=synthetic-hands` to the address and a pretend hand plays.

Thoremin's [home page](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/) opens nothing by itself: from there you pick the camera instruments or the [listening games](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/play/), which use only the microphone.

### Pick something to try

- **[Field instruments](#areas-field)**: play the air like a theremin, with fingers, faces and open hands shaping the sound.
- **[Air instruments](#areas-air)**: strike a drum, pluck a bass, strum a guitar, blow a flute, at nothing.
- **[Chords and harmony](#areas-chords)**: point at chords on a map or a wheel instead of notes.
- **[Sign instruments](#areas-sign)**: fingerspell and see or hear the letters.
- **[Conduct and dance](#conduct-and-dance)**: the [Conductor](#tools-conductor) follows your beat; the [Song](#tools-song) follows your pulse.
- **[Teach it yourself](#teach-it-yourself)**: [Your faces](#tools-faces), [Your hand shapes](#tools-hand-shapes) and [Your poses](#tools-poses) learn the faces, shapes and poses you can make.

**How the guide is arranged**

Each page starts with what to do, in three lines. "How it works" and "Settings" are folded away until you want them. The [instruments](#instruments) and the [tools](#tools) each have a page; the [settings](#settings) page explains every section of the instrument editor; and [the manual](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/manual.html) lists every knob. The **Export** menu at the top of every page gives you the whole guide as one file: one page of HTML, a PDF (it opens that page to print: choose Save as PDF) or Markdown.

**Instruments, sounds and saving**

An **instrument** is a named saved set of every setting: scales, sounds, ranges, finger routing, what the face does, what is drawn on the video. A **sound** is just the timbre (a bell, a pad, a reed). Open the instrument list to browse them: star the ones you like, give them your own tags, and hover one for a short summary. Editing an instrument marks it changed; **Save** asks before it overwrites the instrument; to keep both, type a name in the list's "Save current sound as..." box and press **Save**. Your working state survives a reload.

## Everything

![Everything, as it looks on screen](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/everything.jpg)

### What to do

1. Play notes with your wrist.
2. Bring the index, middle and ring fingertips toward the thumb for brightness, vibrato and pan; pinch the pinky to jump an octave.
3. Smile and change your expression to change the timbre.

Everything at once: wrist notes, all four fingers routed, the open hand brightening the tone, and the face shaping the timbre.

**How it works**

It is a maximal setting of the same controls the other field instruments use, so it is a good place to see how much one instrument can hold. Switch things off in **Hand** and **Face** until it plays the way you like.

Its settings: [Sound](#settings--sound), [Hand](#settings--hand), [Face](#settings--face). Every knob: [the manual](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/manual.html).

## Finger FX

![Finger FX, as it looks on screen](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/finger-fx.jpg)

### What to do

1. Hold a hand up so all your fingers show.
2. Bring the index fingertip toward the thumb for a brighter tone, the middle for vibrato.
3. Bring the ring fingertip toward the thumb to pan, the pinky to bend the pitch.

Each finger is a control, measured by how close its tip is to your thumb. Lines on the video show which finger drives which effect, with its value.

**How it works**

Every finger can be routed to an effect (brightness, vibrato, pan, pitch bend, octave, gate), continuously or as a trigger, with its own sensitivity. This instrument routes the index to brightness, the middle to vibrato, the ring to pan and the pinky to pitch bend.

**Change the routing**

In the editor, open **Hand**, then **Finger effects**. It lists the four fingers: pick an effect for each, continuous or trigger, and how strongly it reacts.

Its settings: [Hand](#settings--hand). Every knob: [the manual](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/manual.html).

## Finger Gate

![Finger Gate, as it looks on screen](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/finger-gate.jpg)

### What to do

1. Hold a hand up. Touch the index finger to the thumb to make a sound.
2. Let go to stop it.
3. Bring the middle fingertip toward the thumb to brighten the tone.

The index finger is a key: touching the thumb opens the sound, letting go closes it.

**How it works**

The index finger is routed to the gate (the volume opens as the fingertip nears the thumb) and the middle finger to brightness. Your hand's position still chooses the note.

Its settings: [Hand](#settings--hand). Every knob: [the manual](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/manual.html).

## Glass Bells

![Glass Bells, as it looks on screen](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/glass-bells.jpg)

### What to do

1. Look at the camera and change your expression: smile, open your mouth, raise your brows.
2. Each expression plays a chord that rings as a shimmering arpeggio.
3. You can play with a hand at the same time.

Your face plays chords: the expression decides which chord of the key sounds, and the chord is played as a rising arpeggio.

**How it works**

In **Face**, the mapping *Expression → chord* turns facial expressions into the chords of the key (a smile, a frown, a surprised face each pick a different one). The rendering *Arpeggio ↑* plays the chord's notes one after another at a steady tempo. Your own faces can replace the built-in ones: see [Your faces](#tools-faces).

Its settings: [Face](#settings--face), [Sound](#settings--sound). Every knob: [the manual](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/manual.html).

## Hand-shape Chords

![Hand-shape Chords, as it looks on screen](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/hand-shape-chords.jpg)

### What to do

1. Make a hand shape and hold it a moment: an open hand, a fist, a pointing finger or a pinch.
2. Each shape plays its own chord of the key; a relaxed hand plays none.
3. Your hands' height still plays the melody on top.

Four hand shapes, four chords. It plays a shipped example of a hand-shape categorizer, made from one signer's hands (a recording of fingerspelling by one person, SignAlphaSet, Garg et al., CC BY 4.0), so it reads some hands and not others (an open palm best; a fist or a pointing hand often not): teach it your own shapes in **Your hand shapes** and choose them in [Categorizer chords](#settings--categorizer-chords).

**How it works**

The shape of your hand is compared with the four shapes the example learned; the nearest one, held a moment, chooses the chord. The first shape plays the key's home chord, then the fourth, the fifth and the sixth (the strongest chords first); each can be changed in **Categorizer chords**.

Its settings: [Categorizer chords](#settings--categorizer-chords), [Sound](#settings--sound). Every knob: [the manual](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/manual.html).

## Head Chords

![Head Chords, as it looks on screen](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/head-chords.jpg)

### What to do

1. Turn your head slowly from left to right to move through the key's chords.
2. Open your mouth to sound the chord; close it to rest.
3. Smile to brighten it, raise your eyebrows to add the seventh, nod up or down for the octave.

Your head is a keyboard of chords: where you look picks the chord, your mouth plays it. Your expression does not choose the chord, so you can play it with a straight face.

**How it works**

The head's turn is split into one slice per chord of the key, left to right, so a slow turn walks up the scale's chords. The chord sounds only while your mouth is open. A smile opens the sound and a frown darkens it, raised eyebrows add the seventh, and nodding up or down moves it an octave. Chords are voice-led: each one moves as little as it can from the last. The key and how the chord sounds are in **Face** and **Sound**. Your hands still play the melody on top.

Its settings: [Face](#settings--face), [Sound](#settings--sound). Every knob: [the manual](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/manual.html).

## Pentatonic

![Pentatonic, as it looks on screen](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/pentatonic.jpg)

### What to do

1. Allow the camera, then tap anywhere to start the sound.
2. Hold a hand up: left and right is pitch, up and down is loudness.
3. Raise a second hand for a second voice.

The gentle default. The index fingertip is the note, and the notes are snapped toward a five-note scale, so it is hard to play a wrong one.

**How it works**

Your hand's position is read as a note of the scale (the pentatonic scale in C by default). *Magnetism* decides how hard the pitch snaps to the scale's notes: 0 % glides freely, 100 % jumps from note to note. Each hand is its own voice with its own scale, sound and range.

**Make it yours**

In the editor, **Sound** sets the root note, the scale, the octave range and the sound of each voice; **Hand** sets the magnetism and what the open hand and the pinch do. Save the result as your own instrument.

Its settings: [Sound](#settings--sound), [Hand](#settings--hand). Every knob: [the manual](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/manual.html).

## Physical Theremin

![Physical Theremin, as it looks on screen](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/physical-theremin.jpg)

### What to do

1. Bring your right hand toward the rod on the right of the video: the closer, the higher the pitch.
2. Hold the hand still and open or close your fingers to play intervals: an open hand sounds higher than a fist.
3. Raise your left hand over the loop at the bottom left for more volume; lower it onto the loop for silence.

A theremin that works the way a real one does. Your hand and the rod are the two plates of an invisible capacitor, and the pitch comes from how much they couple, so both where your hand is and its shape play the note.

**How it works**

On a real theremin your hand and the pitch antenna form a tiny capacitor, a fraction of a picofarad with the arm stretched out and a few picofarads close by. That capacitor detunes a radio oscillator, and the difference between it and a second, fixed oscillator is the note you hear. This instrument computes the same thing from the camera: each part of your hand (the palm and every finger) adds its area divided by its distance to the rod.

So the field is crowded near the rod: each octave up takes half the distance of the octave below. **Linearity** (in the settings) straightens it out, the way the coil inside a modern theremin does, so equal moves give more even intervals.

Opening a finger adds its area to the plate, so it raises the pitch. That is **aerial fingering**, the technique Clara Rockmore made famous: keep the hand in one place and play the intervals with the fingers. Here a fist sounds about five semitones below an open hand at the same spot. A palm turned to face the rod couples more than one seen edge-on.

The left hand works the volume loop the same way: the nearer it is, the more it detunes the volume circuit and the quieter the sound, down to silence on the loop. With the left hand out of view the sound plays at full volume, as a real theremin does with no hand near the loop. Lower your right hand out of view to stop.

**Vibrato** is a quick, small rocking of the wrist, five or six times a second. **Staccato** is a quick dip of the left hand onto the loop and back.

**Assistance**

A real theremin has no frets: the note is wherever your hand is, and it slides between notes. **Assistance** adds help a little at a time, from 0 (a pure theremin with no help at all) up to 1:

- **Pitch correction** pulls the pitch toward the nearest note. Low assistance pulls gently toward every semitone; from the middle up it pulls toward the notes of the instrument's scale (set in the Sound section). At full strength it locks onto the note, and a hand held near the border between two notes stays on one of them.
- **Smoothing** quiets the small shake the camera sees in a still hand, while still following a real move.
- **Vibrato aid** notices a real wrist vibrato and keeps it through the correction (a plain pitch correction would flatten it), a little deeper.

The four helpers follow the assistance level until you move one of them yourself; then they are yours, and "Back to the level's defaults" hands them back. Assistance at 0 always turns all of them off.

**Settings**

**Physical theremin** in the editor: on or off, the assistance and its four helpers, the sound and the volume. Under **Antennas and circuits**: linearity, how sensitive the rod is, the zero beat (the low hum with no hand near; 0 is silence), the pitch circuit's capacitance (a larger one gives a narrower range), how close a hand can come, where the rod stands and how high the loop is, how sharply the loop silences, what happens with the left hand out of view, and whether the antennas and the note readout are drawn on the video.

Its settings: [Physical theremin](#settings--physical-theremin). Every knob: [the manual](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/manual.html).

## Pitch Bender

![Pitch Bender, as it looks on screen](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/pitch-bender.jpg)

### What to do

1. Play a note by moving your hand.
2. Pinch the index finger toward the thumb to bend the pitch up.
3. Release to let it spring back down.

A whole-tone bend upward under your fingertips, like a guitar string.

**How it works**

The distance between the index fingertip and the thumb is routed to pitch bend. A full bend is a whole tone, reached a little before your fingertip touches the thumb.

## Pose Chords

![Pose Chords, as it looks on screen](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/pose-chords.jpg)

### What to do

1. Stand back so the camera sees your whole body.
2. Hold a pose a moment: both arms up, the left arm out, the right arm out, or a crouch.
3. Each pose plays its own chord of the key; standing at rest plays none.

Four poses, four chords. It plays a shipped example of a pose categorizer, made from the app's own scripted body, so it may not read you well: teach it your own poses in **Your poses** and choose them in [Categorizer chords](#settings--categorizer-chords).

**How it works**

The body camera reads your pose, and the nearest of the four poses the example learned, held a moment, chooses the chord. The first pose plays the key's home chord, then the fourth, the fifth and the sixth; each can be changed in **Categorizer chords**. It needs the body camera, so it starts a little slower than a hands-only instrument.

Its settings: [Categorizer chords](#settings--categorizer-chords), [Sound](#settings--sound). Every knob: [the manual](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/manual.html).

## Pulse Organ

![Pulse Organ, as it looks on screen](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/pulse-organ.jpg)

### What to do

1. Look at the camera and change your expression.

## Chord Map + Face

## Chord Map Paths

![Chord Map Paths, as it looks on screen](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/chord-map-paths.jpg)

### What to do

3. Read the words around your chord to see what each move does, and watch the torus above light up the loop of the move you just made.

## Chord Map

![Chord Map, as it looks on screen](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/chord-map.jpg)

### What to do

1. Hold your left hand over a chord on the map: that chord sounds and keeps sounding.
2. Raise and lower your right hand to play a melody on the chord's notes.
3. Move the left hand to a neighbouring chord: the change is smooth, the shared notes held.

A key's seven chords laid out on the video like a map. Your left hand chooses the chord, your right hand sings over it.

**How it works**

The map is a honeycomb of the key's chords (C major by default, the key the Sound section sets). Each chord's six neighbours are the other six chords, so any change is one short reach away: across is a fourth, the diagonals a third and a second. A chord is chosen when your hand's palm has been over it for a moment, and it stays chosen a little after your hand leaves the map, so a shaky hand at a border never flickers between two chords. Each change is played the way a pianist would, moving each voice as little as it can.

Your right hand plays the melody: its height picks a note from the chord, an octave above it. Hold the hand upright for the chord's own notes, tilt it for a pentatonic, tilt it further for the whole scale.

**Settings**

**Chord map** in the editor: the layout (the honeycomb map, one row by fifths, or one row in scale order); hold or strum; which hand points and with what (the palm or a fingertip); how big the chords are drawn; the melody's notes and volume; seventh chords; the words on each move (the interval, or what the move does); a known chord path to follow, drawn ahead of your chord as a trail; whether the map is drawn on the video; and what keeps the beat for arpeggios (the Chord sound's tempo, or the song loaded in the Song tool). A new chord can also wait for the next beat, so changes land in time. The key and the way a held chord sounds (sustained, arpeggiated, its tempo) are in **Sound**.

Its settings: [Chord map](#settings--chord-map), [Sound](#settings--sound). Every knob: [the manual](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/manual.html).

## Tonnetz Wheel

![Tonnetz Wheel, as it looks on screen](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/tonnetz-wheel.jpg)

### What to do

1. Hold a chord with your left hand.
2. Push toward a move and hold.
3. These moves leave the key: each changes one or two notes of the chord.

The chord wheel with moves that wander out of the key, the way film music slides between distant chords.

**How it works**

The Tonnetz is a map of chords where neighbours share two of their three notes. Each move here changes one note (or two) and keeps the rest: C major to C minor, C major to A minor, C major to E minor, and so on. Because no key holds them in, a few moves take you far from where you started while every single step still sounds close. Each move undoes itself, so pushing the same way twice brings you back.

**Settings**

In **Chord wheel**: the move set (the key's moves or the Tonnetz's), and everything else as on the [Chord Wheel](#instruments-chord-wheel).

Its settings: [Chord wheel](#settings--chord-wheel). Every knob: [the manual](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/manual.html).

## Wheel Walk

![Wheel Walk, as it looks on screen](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/wheel-walk.jpg)

### What to do

1. Hold your left hand on a chord.
2. Push toward a move and keep holding it out.
3. The wheel keeps making that move, one per beat: up walks the cycle of fourths.

Walk a chain of chords on the beat by holding a push.

**How it works**

Instead of one change per push, a push held out keeps making the same move on every beat. Pushing toward the fourth walks all seven chords of the key and comes home. **Settings**

In **Chord wheel**: walking on the beat, and every how many beats a move is made. The tempo and the arpeggio pattern are in **Sound**.

## Sign Wheel

![Sign Wheel, as it looks on screen](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/sign-wheel.jpg)

## Drawn Pieces

![Drawn Pieces, as it looks on screen](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/drawn-pieces.jpg)

### What to do

## Your faces

![Your faces, open in the app](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/guide/pics/tool-faces.jpg)

### What to do

1. Open Tools, then Your faces, then Record my faces.
2. Stay relaxed while it learns your rest, then make your faces one after another.
3. Press **Stop and carve**, name the groups it finds, and press Play it.

Teach the instrument YOUR faces instead of fixed expressions. It records you, finds the groups, and plays chords from them.

**How a take goes**

1. **Record.** *Record my faces* opens a screen with live feature bars (pin the ones you care about). *Start capturing* first asks for a few seconds of rest (a ring fills while it learns your rest), then you make your faces one after another. A ring shows when a face is held and taken. **Space** pauses and resumes.
2. **Bursts and names.** Hold **B** (or the button) and every frame becomes one hold; type a name first and it goes straight into the category of that name.
3. **Light.** If the room is too dark, a line says so: add light, or record again in this light.

**Sort the groups**

Stop, and the holds are carved into groups. Pick how many with the *How many* slider, then fix them: drag stills between groups, press a group's number, **N** for Neutral or **S** to set aside, **Delete** to drop a hold, merge or split a group, name them, undo and redo. Whatever you move is *pinned*, so changing the number later keeps your choices. **Neutral** is a class you teach ("do nothing"); one switch decides whether an untaught face reads as Neutral or as the nearest group.

Each group shows dots for how clearly it differs from its nearest neighbour, and plain hints when two are close. *Show a map of the holds* draws every hold as a dot, with a red ring for the face the camera sees now, and lets you drag a box around holds to select them.

**Try it, save it, play it**

*Trying* shows what the instrument reads live, with a margin meter for how deep inside a group you are. *Quiz me* names each group twice in random order; the *Everyday check* counts accidental fires in a minute of doing nothing. Models are saved in your browser, several per person, and export and import as files. *Play it* makes the model play chords on the instrument; an instrument can pick its model in the editor's Face categorizer section. A saved model can also fire commands (see [Gestures](#tools-gestures)).

Its settings: [Face categorizer](#settings--face-categorizer).

## Conduct and dance

Your whole body can lead the music. Open the [Conductor](#tools-conductor) and the [Song](#tools-song) tools to try these; each page there starts with what to do.

### Conduct a score

Load a MIDI or MusicXML score (a demo is built in), press **Start conducting** and beat time in front of the camera. A sampled orchestra plays and follows your tempo and your loudness. You choose which parts play, and the orchestra is optional: tick **Play with the orchestra** and it downloads the first time.

**Hand or body**

By default your beating hand's strokes are the beats. Choose **body** and several joints (arm and trunk) are read: start, hold, fermata and cut-off come from your body, and your other hand can shape loudness and staccato. *Learn my timing* measures how far each joint lags a click and keeps the ones it measured clearly, so the beat lands when you meant it. Body conducting needs most of your upper body in view.

**Start, hold and stop with poses**

A pose model you taught in [Your poses](#tools-poses) can start, hold and cut off the conductor: raise your arm and keep still to get ready, then give the upbeat. See [Your poses](#tools-poses) for how to teach "ready", "hold" and "cut off".

### Dance the orchestra

Choose **Dance the orchestra** and the score follows a dancer instead of a conductor: the tempo follows your stride, a written fermata holds for one cycle of yours, and the piece loops while you keep dancing. Turn body tracking on in the editor's [Body](#settings--body) section first, or let the conductor ask for it.

### A song that follows you

In the [Song](#tools-song) tool, load a song file. Its beat is tracked in your browser. Tick *Follow the dancer* and the song's tempo follows your pulse within a range you set (10 percent by default), whether you bounce on every beat or every other. *Start on your step* lands a beat of the song on your step when you press Play. Pausing the song keeps the lock.

**Conduct a recording**

The song itself can follow your conducting: you choose which beat of the song your first beat lands on, and on a hold the song can fade and slow down instead of cutting. Both are off until you set them.

### Conduct the chords

[Conduct the Chords](#instruments-conduct-the-chords) plays the chords of a piece, voice-led and arpeggiated, on your beat.

## Teach it yourself

Instead of fixed expressions, shapes and poses, you can show thoremin YOURS and it learns to tell them apart. One tool, three ways to use it:

- [Your faces](#tools-faces): the faces you can make. Play chords with them, or fire commands.
- [Your hand shapes](#tools-hand-shapes): the shapes of one hand. They can be the air guitar's chords, or fire commands.
- [Your poses](#tools-poses): your body's poses. They can start, hold and stop the conductor.

### How it goes, in five steps

1. **Record.** Press *Record my faces* (or hand shapes, or poses). Stay relaxed for a few seconds while it learns your rest, then make your faces one after another.
2. **Sort.** Stop, and your holds are grouped. Pick how many groups, drag holds between them, name them. Undo and redo are there.
3. **Check.** Each group shows how clearly it differs from its neighbours, and says in plain words what to do when two are close.
4. **Try it.** *Trying* shows live what it reads. *Quiz me* names each group twice, in random order, and shows which you can find again.
5. **Use it.** Save it, then press *Play it*, choose it in the editor's [Face categorizer](#settings--face-categorizer) or [Pose categorizer](#settings--pose-categorizer) section, or bind it to commands in [Gestures](#tools-gestures).

**Good takes**

Use even light on your face ("Too dark" shows when the room is too dim). Make each face clearly different from the others and hold it for a moment. Teach "Neutral" (a relaxed face that should play nothing) so an untaught face is not mistaken for a group.


<a id="settings"></a>

## Settings

The instrument editor (the Edit button next to an instrument) is made of sections. Each page about an instrument says which ones matter for it. Every knob is in [the manual](https://apps.thorwhalen.com/thoremin/manual.html); this page says what each section is for.

<a id="settings--sound"></a>

### Sound

Per voice (right and left): the root note, the scale (major, minor, pentatonic, blues, chromatic...), the octave range, the base octave and the sound (the timbre); the master volume sits at the top. *Sync both hands* makes the left voice copy the right.

<a id="settings--hand"></a>

### Hand

Where the note comes from (the index fingertip or the steadier wrist), the maximum volume, the stereo pan spread, a handedness dwell that stops the camera swapping left and right, and what the open hand, the pinch, the closed fist and the position on screen do. Each finger can also be routed to an effect: brightness, vibrato, pan, pitch bend, octave or gate, continuous or as a trigger, with its own sensitivity.

<a id="settings--face"></a>

### Face

What the face does: nothing; *timbre* (a smile brightens, an open mouth adds vibrato); *chord* (an expression plays a chord of the key); or *controls* (a deliberate head, jaw or brow pose plays a chord). Plus the chord sound, volume, voicing (open, bass + triad, close, shell, power, voice-led), the rendering (sustained pad, strum, arpeggio up, down or up-down, pulse, alberti) and which chord each expression plays.

<a id="settings--overlay"></a>

### Overlay

What is drawn on the video: the video itself, the hand landmarks, the control markers, pitch and scale guides, chord names, the keyboard strip, the face mesh, the finger lines and bars. Switch things off for a clean picture.

<a id="settings--keyboard"></a>

### Keyboard

The keyboard shortcuts, listed. See also [Commands](#tools-commands).

<a id="settings--air-drum"></a>

### Air drum

Switch the drum on; choose which hand strikes, each hand's drum sound (right: kick, left: snare), the stick point (wrist, index fingertip or stick tip), how fast a stroke must be to sound at full volume, how far ahead a hit is committed (Lead), how much the timing is pulled to the beat; the pads (move, resize, stack, give each a sound); whether the pads and sticks are drawn on the video; saved pad layouts; and the pattern trainer.

<a id="settings--air-bass"></a>

### Air bass

Switch the bass on; choose the plucking hand and the point that plucks, how far apart the hands are at the highest and the lowest note (in palm widths), and the volume. The notes themselves are the Sound section's scale.

<a id="settings--air-guitar"></a>

### Air guitar

Switch the guitar on; choose the strumming hand and point, the volume, teach your chord shapes (enrol them one by one) or run the chord sequence trainer.

<a id="settings--air-flute"></a>

### Air flute

Switch the flute on; choose how a note is started (your blowing mouth, or fingers only: a held fingering sounds on its own), the volume, teach your fingerings and your blowing mouth, pick a wind instrument's fingering chart as a guide, show the breath cue, and run the sequence trainer.

<a id="settings--chord-map"></a>

### Chord map

The chord map's own settings; the key is the **Sound** section's root and scale, and the way a held chord sounds (sustained or arpeggiated, its voicing and tempo) is there too.

- **Layout**: the honeycomb map, where every chord's neighbours are the other six; one row by fifths; or one row in scale order. **Letters** keeps the map in place when the key changes, so a new key lights up the chords it changes instead of moving everything.
- **Hold or strum**: hold a chord and play a melody over it, or strum it like the air guitar.
- **Which hand points**, and with what: the palm or a fingertip. **Chord size**: bigger chords are easier to hit, smaller ones show more of the map.
- **Move captions**: the words on each move from the chord you hold. **Intervals** says the move in scale steps (4th up, 3rd down); **Computed** says what it does: how many notes it keeps, whether it turns darker or brighter, and how expected it is after the chord you hold (Piston's table of usual progressions); **Yours** says what you said when you rated the move (below), and nothing on a move you have not rated yet.
- **Rate this move**: a practice that plays a move between two chords (C major's for major keys, A minor's for minor) and asks where it sits for you, from dark to bright and from settled to tense. Press Play, place the two sliders, Save rating; the next move is one you have rated least. A move rated in C is the same move in every major key (I to vi is I to vi anywhere), and it keeps your last twelve ratings, so its words follow you if you hear it differently later. Your ratings stay in this browser.
- **Trail to follow**: a well-known chord path (Pachelbel's Canon, the Romanesca, the circle of fifths, the Andalusian cadence, the fifties progression...) drawn ahead of your chord as a dashed trail. Play the next chord on it and the trail moves on; wander off and it waits where you left it. **Trail length** is how many chords ahead it shows.
- **Torus**: the seven chords drawn on a slowly turning ring-shaped surface above the map. On it every chord touches all six others, and the three loops are the three kinds of move (by a second, a third, a fourth), the same three directions as the honeycomb. The loop of the move you just made lights up. **Torus turns** sets its speed (0 holds it still).
- **Melody**: on or off, its notes (the chord's tones, a pentatonic, the whole scale, or chosen by tilting the hand), its volume. **Seventh chords** turns the key's chords into sevenths. Also: draw the map on the video, the chords' share of the screen and the strum volume.
- **Face**: whether your face colours the chords (a smile major, a frown minor; an open mouth with raised brows adds a seventh, then a ninth), and whether it is read against your face at rest.
- **What chooses the chords**: your hand, a MIDI keyboard (Chrome or Edge), the microphone, or your signs: fingerspell a letter A to G and that letter's chord in the key lights up while your other hand plays over it (turn on **Sign language** too). **Chords from**: the key's seven, or a pentatonic's own five.
- **Timing**: what keeps the beat (the Chord sound's tempo, a song loaded in the Song tool, or the conductor while you conduct), and whether a new chord waits for the next beat.
- **Grid on the video**: draw the map over the video, or play it undrawn.

<a id="settings--chord-wheel"></a>

### Chord wheel

The chord wheel's own settings; the key and how chords sound are in **Sound**.

- **Moves**: the key's six moves, or the Tonnetz's, which leave the key one or two changed notes at a time.
- **Steering**: your hand, your face, or both, your signs, or your poses; which hand and which point of it; the wheel's size; and how far to push and how long to hold before a hand move is made. With signs, a fingerspelled letter A to G goes to that letter's chord in the key, and the next letter goes straight on (turn on **Sign language** too). With your poses, your first pose makes the top move, the next pose the next move clockwise (choose the poses in **Pose categorizer**).
- **Order**: the moves in a fixed place, or the likeliest next chord on top. **Surprise** brings rarer moves to the top, from this setting, your other hand's height, or your face.
- **Tension**: a number under each chord saying how far it is from home, and which model measures it (the models disagree, so the model is always named).
- **Walk on the beat**: a push held out keeps making that move, every one to four beats.
- **Timing**: what keeps the beat for arpeggios and walks (the Chord sound's tempo, a song loaded in the Song tool, or the conductor while you conduct).

<a id="settings--chord-chart"></a>

### Chord chart

For conducting a piece's chords (it needs the **Conductor**, which chooses the piece and the parts).

- **Play the chord chart**: on or off.
- **A chord every**: how much of the piece is read as one chord, every beat for fast harmony, two beats or a bar for slow.
- How the chord sounds (its voicing and arpeggio) is the chord sound in **Sound**.

<a id="settings--sign-language"></a>

### Sign language

Switch the ASL reader on, include the movement signs (J, Z and the words), set how confident and how smooth the reading must be, and choose whether A to G play chords, whether the chord holds between letters, and the key, sound and volume of those chords.

<a id="settings--drawn-pieces"></a>

### Drawn pieces

Switch between shaping (a pinch draws or moves a piece) and playing (the fingertips strike, touch and pluck them), set the volume and the Shape new pieces start with, and give each piece its Shape, Feel, Tone, sound and note. Under Advanced: how hard a stroke must be to count as a hit, and whether only pieces near your hand's distance from the camera can be struck.

<a id="settings--physical-theremin"></a>

### Physical theremin

Play the theremin through its physics: switch it on, set the assistance (0 is a pure theremin; up from there it adds pitch correction toward the semitones or the scale, smoothing and a vibrato aid, each of which you can tune yourself), choose the sound and volume. Under Antennas and circuits: the linearity of the pitch field, the rod's sensitivity, the zero beat, the pitch circuit's capacitance, the closest approach, where the rod and the loop are, how sharply the loop silences, what a missing left hand does, and what is drawn on the video.

<a id="settings--midi"></a>

### MIDI

Send the voices you hear to an external instrument or a music program. Off by default; it needs a browser with Web MIDI (Chrome and Edge, not Safari). A device plugged in later appears after you switch MIDI off and on.

<a id="settings--generative"></a>

### Generative

Steer an AI music model with your gestures. Off by default; it needs your own Google API key (the Assistant's key serves both), and says plainly when it cannot connect.

<a id="settings--body"></a>

### Body

Turn on body tracking (the most expensive thing the app can run, so it is off until you ask), choose the model (lite or full), and route a body feature to a sound aspect.

<a id="settings--conductor"></a>

### Conductor

Switch conducting on, choose the piece, the orchestra and its parts, conduct with the hand or the body, where the loudness comes from, and how closely the score follows you.

<a id="settings--face-categorizer"></a>

### Face categorizer

Choose which of your saved face models the instrument plays, and which chord each of its groups plays.

<a id="settings--pose-categorizer"></a>

### Pose categorizer

Choose which of your saved pose models the instrument plays, and whether it starts, holds and cuts off the conductor.

<a id="settings--categorizer-chords"></a>

### Categorizer chords

The chords your own categories play, one degree of the key per group. Besides your own hand-shape and pose categorizers, **Chords from** lists two shipped examples (a few hand shapes, a few poses), which the Hand-shape Chords and Pose Chords instruments play.


<a id="troubleshooting"></a>

## Troubleshooting

### No sound

Browsers will not make sound until you tap. Press **Tap to play** once. If the status still says it is waiting, check that the tab is not muted and the device volume is up. The **m** key mutes everything the instrument plays: press it again.

### The camera does not start

- Allow the camera when the browser asks. If you said no, the address bar's camera icon lets you change it.
- Close other programs that use the camera (video calls). Only one can have it.
- The page must be opened from its real address (`https://`), not from a file.
- Without a camera, add `?slot.source=synthetic-hands` to the address to try the instruments with a pretend hand.

### It does not see my hand, or jitters

- Light the room from the front or the side, not from behind you. The face tools say "Too dark: add light, or record again in this light" when it is too dim.
- Keep your hand in view and a little away from the camera, with the palm toward it.
- The **Wrist Theremin** instrument is steadier than the fingertip.
- With two hands close together the second can be missed; move them apart.

### Browsers

Chrome and Edge work best. Safari and Firefox work for playing, with gaps: no folder picker for saving takes (they download a zip), and Safari has no Web MIDI. The first time you open the sign-language reader it downloads a little.

### MIDI

Needs Chrome or Edge. Switch **MIDI** on in the editor, then choose the port. A device you plug in later appears after you switch MIDI off and on. [Play Along (MIDI)](#instruments-play-along-midi)) needs a MIDI keyboard connected before you open it.

### Microphone

[Play Along (microphone)](#instruments-play-along-microphone)) asks to use the microphone. Allow it; if you said no, the address bar's icon lets you change it. It hears a guitar, a piano or a recording through the speakers; it hears chords best when the sound is clear.

### Latency (the sound comes late)

- Use headphones or a wired speaker: Bluetooth adds a noticeable delay.
- Close other heavy tabs. The hand model is the most expensive part.
- The air instruments predict the hit before the frame that shows it, so they sound on time even at 30 frames a second; a bright, steady picture helps them most.
- Add `?probe=latency` to the address to see where the time goes, stage by stage.

### Something broke

Reloading restores your saved instruments and your working state. If a setting seems wrong, reselecting the instrument reverts it to its saved version.
