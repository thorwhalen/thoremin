Settings

The instrument editor (the Edit button next to an instrument) is made of sections. Each page about an instrument says which ones matter for it. Every knob is in the manual; this page says what each section is for.

Sound

Per voice (right and left): the root note, the scale (major, minor, pentatonic, blues, chromatic...), the octave range, the sound (the timbre) and the volume. Sync with right makes the left voice copy the right.

Hand

Where the note comes from (the index fingertip or the steadier wrist), the magnetism (0 % glides freely, 100 % snaps to the scale), and what the open hand, the pinch, the closed fist and the position on screen do. Each finger can also be routed to an effect: brightness, vibrato, pan, pitch bend, octave or gate, continuous or as a trigger, with its own sensitivity.

Face

What the face does: nothing; timbre (a smile brightens, an open mouth adds vibrato); chord (an expression plays a chord of the key); or controls (a deliberate head, jaw or brow pose plays a chord). Plus the chord sound, volume, voicing (spread, close, shell, power, voice-led), the rendering (sustained, strum, arpeggio up or down, pulse, alberti) and which chord each expression plays.

Overlay

What is drawn on the video: the video itself, the hand landmarks, the control markers, pitch and scale guides, chord names, the keyboard strip, the face mesh, the finger lines and bars. Switch things off for a clean picture.

Keyboard

The keyboard shortcuts, listed. See also Commands.

Air drum

Switch the drum on; choose which hand strikes, each hand's drum sound (right: kick, left: snare), the stick length, how hard a stroke must be to count as a hard hit, how much the timing is pulled to the beat, the volume; the pads (move, resize, stack, give each a sound); whether the pads and sticks are drawn on the video; saved pad layouts; and the pattern trainer.

Air bass

Switch the bass on; choose the plucking hand and the point that plucks, the notes at the near and far ends of the neck, how smooth the neck is, how sensitive the pluck is, and the volume.

Air guitar

Switch the guitar on; choose the strumming hand and point, how spread the strum is, how sensitive the strum is, the volume, and teach your chord shapes (enrol them one by one).

Air flute

Switch the flute on; choose how you blow (with the mouth, or with the face), the volume, teach your fingerings and your blowing mouth, pick a wind instrument's fingering chart as a guide, show the breath cue, and run the sequence trainer.

Chord map

The key (it follows the Sound section's root and scale), the layout (hex lattice, fifths row or scale row), whether you hold a chord or strum it, which notes the melody hand plays, whether the face colours the chords, and whether a MIDI keyboard or the microphone chooses the chords.

Chord wheel

The moves around the wheel (the key's six, or the Tonnetz's), what steers it (the hand or the face), the walk on the beat, and how the likeliest next chord is ranked.

Chord chart

Plays a piece's chords on the conductor's beat. Switch it on with Conduct the Chords.

Sign language

Switch the ASL reader on, and the key, sound and voicing for the letter chords.

MIDI

Send the voices you hear to an external instrument or a music program. Off by default; it needs a browser with Web MIDI (Chrome and Edge, not Safari). A device plugged in later appears after you switch MIDI off and on.

Generative

Steer an AI music model with your gestures. Off by default; it needs your own Google API key (the Assistant's key serves both), and says plainly when it cannot connect.

Body

Turn on body tracking (the most expensive thing the app can run, so it is off until you ask), choose the model (lite or full), and route a body feature to a sound aspect.

Conductor

Switch conducting on, choose the piece, the orchestra and its parts, conduct with the hand or the body, where the loudness comes from, and how closely the score follows you.

Face categorizer

Choose which of your saved face models the instrument plays, and which chord each of its groups plays.

Pose categorizer

Choose which of your saved pose models the instrument plays, and whether it starts, holds and cuts off the conductor.

Categorizer chords

The chords your own categories play, one degree of the key per group.
