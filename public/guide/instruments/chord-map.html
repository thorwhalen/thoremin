Chord Map

What to do

  1. Hold your left hand over a chord on the map: that chord sounds and keeps sounding.
  2. Raise and lower your right hand to play a melody on the chord's notes.
  3. Move the left hand to a neighbouring chord: the change is smooth, the shared notes held.

A key's seven chords laid out on the video like a map. Your left hand chooses the chord, your right hand sings over it.

How it works

The map is a honeycomb of the key's chords (C major by default, the key the Sound section sets). Each chord's six neighbours are the other six chords, so any change is one short reach away: across is a fourth, the diagonals a third and a second. A chord is chosen when your hand's palm has been over it for a moment, and it stays chosen a little after your hand leaves the map, so a shaky hand at a border never flickers between two chords. Each change is played the way a pianist would, moving each voice as little as it can.

Your right hand plays the melody: its height picks a note from the chord, an octave above it. Hold the hand upright for the chord's own notes, tilt it for a pentatonic, tilt it further for the whole scale.

Settings

Chord map in the editor: the layout (the honeycomb map, one row by fifths, or one row in scale order); hold or strum; which hand points and with what (the palm or a fingertip); how big the chords are drawn; the melody's notes, volume and range; seventh chords; the words on each move (the interval, or what the move does); a known chord path to follow, drawn ahead of your chord as a trail; whether the map is drawn on the video; and what keeps the beat for arpeggios (the Chord sound's tempo, or the song loaded in the Song tool). A new chord can also wait for the next beat, so changes land in time. The key and the way a held chord sounds (sustained, arpeggiated, its tempo) are in Sound.

Its settings: Chord map, Sound. Every knob: the manual.
