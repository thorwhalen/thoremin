Chord Map + Face

What to do

  1. Point at a chord with your left hand, as on the Chord Map.
  2. Hold a smile to turn the key major; hold a frown to turn it minor.
  3. Open your mouth and raise your brows to add a seventh, then a ninth.

The chord map, coloured by your face. Your hands still choose the chord; your face changes its colour.

How it works

Your face never chooses a chord: it colours the one your hands hold. A smile held for about a second turns the key to its major (A minor becomes A major), a frown back to minor, and a neutral face plays the key as set. Opening your mouth with raised brows adds tension: first the key's seventh, then its ninth. The face is read against your own face at rest, learned in the first calm seconds you play, so brows that naturally sit high or a shadow that looks like a frown count for nothing. This instrument starts in A minor so the smile has somewhere to go.

Settings

In Chord map: whether the face colours the map, and separately whether it turns the key major or minor and whether it adds sevenths and ninths; how long a smile must be held; and whether the face is read against your rest (on) or against fixed levels. The face model itself is set up in Face.

Its settings: Chord map, Face. Every knob: the manual.
