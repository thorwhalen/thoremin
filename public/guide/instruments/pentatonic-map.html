Pentatonic Map

What to do

  1. Point at a chord with your left hand.
  2. Play a melody with your right hand.
  3. The map holds the five chords made from the pentatonic scale itself.

The chord map for a pentatonic scale's own five chords: open, folky harmony where nothing clashes.

How it works

Instead of a key's seven chords, the map lays out the chords you get by stacking the pentatonic's own notes: in C, a C6, a Gsus4, an Asus4, a C and a Dsus4. These are the suspended, open sounds of folk and pop harmony. The five chords sit on five cells, and every step between neighbours reaches all five. The melody hand plays the same scale, so anything you play over any chord fits.

Settings

In Chord map: the chords can come from the key (seven chords) or from the scale itself (here, five). The scale is chosen in Sound; with a pentatonic or a harmonic minor the map can use the scale's own chords, and any other scale uses its key's seven.

Its settings: Chord map, Sound. Every knob: the manual.
