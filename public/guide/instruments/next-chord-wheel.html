Next-Chord Wheel

What to do

  1. Hold a chord with your left hand.
  2. The likeliest next chord is on top; push up to take it.
  3. Raise your right hand to bring rarer, more surprising chords to the top.

A wheel that turns to put the most usual next chord on top, with how far each chord is from home written under it.

How it works

The wheel ranks the key's moves from the chord you are on by how often composers make them, so the top move is always the expected one: pushing up from C plays the classic C, F, G, C. Under each chord a number says how far it is from the key's home chord, so you can see tension rise and fall. Raising your right hand (or the surprise setting) brings the less usual moves to the top, where music starts to sound fresh.

Settings

In Chord wheel: the order of the moves (fixed, or likeliest on top), how surprising the top moves are and what sets it (the setting, your other hand's height, or your face), and the tension shown under each chord with the model that measures it (they disagree, so the model is always named).

Its settings: Chord wheel. Every knob: the manual.
